# Co-Origin-Verifiable-Credentials

> The proposes a credential co-generation and verification channel orthogonalization design for digital driver's licenses in heterogeneous verifiable credential ecosystems: at the issuance stage, an authoritative issuer natively generates an SD-JWT VC and an ISO/IEC 18013-5 mDoc from the same attribute source and binds both to a deterministically computed credential reference, ref; at the presentation stage, remote verification uses OpenID4VP with SD-JWT VC, while proximity verification uses OpenID4VP proximity with mDoc, with no format intermediary introduced; the two channels share a field-confirmation interface, and status tokens are bound to the verifier nonce and session identifier sid to suppress cross-session reuse and cross-verifier correlation. T
> he paper formalizes system execution as a state machine, event traces, and induction over reachable states; defines and argues for invariants including source consistency, disclosure minimality, session locality, and channel independence; and characterizes deployment feasibility as a conjunction of jurisdictional external constraints and internal system constraints, analyzing the decomposability of the feasible region, monotonicity, parameter sensitivity, and the existence of an optimal deployment-cost solution. Finally, taking the digital driver's license as the first complete instance, it discusses the design's applicability to scenarios such as identity and qualification proof, finance and credit, and assets and permissions, as well as inapplicability counterexamples.
> The conclusion is that the design satisfies specification consistency under explicit assumptions and is conditionally deployable subject to jurisdiction-specific constraints; its value lies not in eliminating ecosystem divergence but in making the consequences of that divergence computable and auditable.
